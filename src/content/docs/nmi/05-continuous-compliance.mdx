> *In this page:* the monitor, the verdict engine, the evidence
> streams, and the re-judgment path. What continuous compliance means
> for an instrument owner and for an authority.

---

## 1. The monitor

A certified twin is watched, not trusted. The monitor binds the twin
endpoint and cycles on a schedule and on state change: it reads the
served registers, and it judges each read against the instrument’s
certified promises (the accuracy envelope, the declared
characteristics, the operational conditions). The cycle produces
verdicts, and the verdicts accrue into the instrument’s compliance
stream.

The monitor’s rules are the Recommendation’s own: the same constraints
the type evaluation judged at certification time, now judged against
live service.

## 2. What a judgment can say

The verdict engine’s live vocabulary is deliberately three-valued:

- **Pass.** The served value holds the promise, with the serve-time
  freshness proven.
- **Fail.** The served value violates the promise (a drift beyond the
  envelope, a characteristic out of its declared range).
- **Indeterminate.** The engine cannot judge: the twin stopped
  serving, serves stale, or the reference channel’s provenance is
  insufficient. Indeterminate is not a fail; it is the honest answer
  when the basis is missing, and it carries health events so the
  outage is visible.

A silent pass is the one outcome the engine refuses: a value that
cannot prove its freshness is never reported as passing.

## 3. The evidence trail

Every judgment leaves a record, and the records chain:

- **Probe records** (from twin certification): the channel, the
  reference value, the served value, the delta, the verdict.
- **Verdict stream** (from the monitor): each cycle’s judgment with
  its basis (the served registers it judged from) and the definition
  version it judged with.
- **Health events** (from the endpoint): outages, staleness, recovery.
- **Lifecycle events** (from the certificate): flags, suspensions,
  reinstatements, re-judgments.

An auditor can walk the chain from the clause, through the certified
promise, to last quarter’s verdict history, to this morning’s batch
records. The trail is the platform’s runtime, not a report generated
after the fact.

## 4. Re-judgment

When the basis changes (a repaired endpoint, a corrected register, a
renewed probe), the chain re-judges: the same promises, the same
engine, the corrected evidence. The re-judgment is recorded as its own
event with provenance, and the certificate’s lifecycle reflects it
(suspension, reinstatement). Nothing is edited silently; the history
shows both the original judgment and its correction.

## 5. For the authority

For a legal-metrology authority, continuous compliance is the point of
the SMART instrument: certification stops being a point-in-time act
and becomes a surveilled state. The authority reads the same verdict
chain the instrument owner sees, judges from the same definitions, and
can re-run any judgment from its recorded basis.
